How it works

While the line beats, nothing happens. When it stops, the plan runs, in three steps.

  1. I

    Set the plan.

    Name your heirs and their shares, mirror your will or trust, and add the chains you hold on. About ten minutes.

  2. II

    Stay alive.

    A pulse key sends a heartbeat: from a small daemon, or from the phone app you pair with a QR code. A guardian you name can pause the vault once per heartbeat cycle. Reminders are coming soon.

  3. III

    It arrives.

    If the heartbeats stop, the contract pays each heir on every chain and disarms any agent you ran. Your heirs sign in.

The security model, in plain words.

Every key does one thing. None of them can change who gets what.

WhoCanCannot
The pulse key Send a heartbeat. Move funds, change heirs, or read anything private.
The agent key Call agent_pay, only within the spending policy you set. If you run an AI agent, this is its key. Pay a receiver outside the allowlist, or exceed the per-payment and daily limits.
The contract Hold the plan. At settlement, pay heirs, request an MPC signature for each foreign-chain payout, then delete the pulse and agent keys. Move assets outside the configured plan.
A keeper anyone can be one Supply the nonce and gas that settlement needs. Change who gets what.
A thief with your device Send a heartbeat, which only says you are still here. Anything useful. The device holds only a pulse key.

For attorneys

Dead Man’s Wallet is a RUFADAA-style online tool that your client configures. The client names heirs and shares, chooses the chains, and sets the proof-of-life interval, working from the distribution their will or trust specifies. The contract executes that configuration. It does not interpret or amend the governing document.

It is non-custodial: no party, including us, holds the client’s keys or can move assets outside the configured plan. The plan lives on-chain, so a fiduciary can read exactly what was configured and when it last changed.

Counsel can reference the tool in the instrument. The clause is a starting point only.

Sample trust clause

Sample. Review with counsel.

Digital asset distribution tool. The Settlor may configure an online tool known as Dead Man’s Wallet (the “Vault”) to hold or distribute digital assets. The Trustee is authorized to rely on the distribution configured in the Vault as of the date of the Settlor’s incapacity or death, to the extent it is consistent with this instrument, and to access, manage, and transfer on-chain assets associated with the Vault as necessary to administer the trust. In the event of any inconsistency between the Vault’s configuration and this instrument, this instrument controls, and the Trustee may take such steps as are needed to give effect to it.

Questions

What if I am in the hospital?

Choose an interval and a grace period that suit your life: up to ten years between heartbeats, plus missed beats of grace. A guardian you name can pause the vault: one pause per heartbeat cycle, capped at a length you set, and the owner can name no guardian at all. Reminders after a missed heartbeat are coming soon; they are not in the hackathon build.

Is this a will?

No. It is not a will or a trust. It executes the distribution your will or trust specifies, so configure it with your estate attorney and keep the two consistent.

What is a heartbeat?

A small signed message from your pulse key saying you are still here. It is the proof of life. Nothing happens while heartbeats keep arriving.

Which chains?

NEAR, Base, Monad and Solana, the last three through NEAR Chain Signatures. The demo uses Base Sepolia, Monad testnet and Solana devnet. Assets must be held at the vault’s derived addresses to be included.

Can my heirs see my balance?

Not through the app. They see nothing until settlement. NEAR is a public ledger, though, so the plan and balances in the contract are visible to anyone who looks up the vault on-chain.

What does it cost?

The hackathon version runs on testnet and is free. Mainnet pricing has not been set; the waitlist hears first.
