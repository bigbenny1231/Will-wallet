How it works, in three acts

I. Alive

While you are alive, nothing happens.

A pulse key sends a heartbeat on the interval you choose, from a small daemon or from your phone. It can only say you are still here. A guardian you name can pause the vault once per cycle.

II. Silence

When the beats stop, the vault waits.

Miss a heartbeat and the seal turns amber. You set the grace: from minutes to years. Only if the silence outlasts it can anyone start settlement, and the keeper who does earns a small bounty.

III. Settlement

When you are not, this executes.

Five steps, in order, each one a NEAR transaction anyone can push forward. On testnet the foreign payouts are signed; none has arrived yet, because the source addresses are unfunded.

      The security model, in plain words.

      Every key does one thing. None of them can change who gets what, except, on testnet, the deployer key, which can still upgrade the contract.

      WhoCanCannot
      The pulse key Send a heartbeat. Move funds, change heirs, or read anything private.
      The agent key Call agent_pay, only within the spending policy you set. If you run an AI agent, this is its key. Pay a receiver outside the allowlist, or exceed the per-payment and daily limits.
      The contract Hold the plan. At settlement, pay heirs, delete the pulse and agent keys, then request an MPC signature for each foreign-chain payout. Move assets outside the configured plan, as deployed.
      A keeper anyone can be one Supply the fees, gas limit and Solana blockhash that settlement needs. The contract sets the nonce. Change who gets what.
      A thief with your device Send heartbeats, which only say you are still here. That can postpone settlement until you remove the key. Move funds or change heirs. The device holds only a pulse key.
      The deployer key testnet build Upgrade the contract, and so move any vault’s funds. Nothing is out of its reach. Mainnet would remove it or put upgrades behind a DAO.

      For attorneys

      Dead Man’s Wallet is a tool your client configures. The client names heirs and shares, chooses the chains, and sets the proof-of-life interval, working from the distribution their will or trust specifies. The contract executes that configuration. It does not interpret or amend the governing document. Whether it counts as an “online tool” under RUFADAA, or under any state’s law, is a question for counsel; we make no such claim.

      No person holds keys to the vault’s foreign-chain addresses, and the deployed code moves assets only as configured. In this testnet build the deployer’s key can still upgrade the contract, so it is not yet free of our control. The plan lives on-chain, so a fiduciary can read what was configured and the transactions that changed it.

      Questions

      What if I am in the hospital?

      Choose an interval and a grace period that suit your life: up to ten years between heartbeats, plus missed beats of grace. A guardian you name can pause the vault: one pause per heartbeat cycle, capped at a length you set, and the owner can name no guardian at all. Reminders after a missed heartbeat are coming soon; they are not in the hackathon build.

      Is this a will?

      No. It is not a will or a trust. It executes the distribution your will or trust specifies, so configure it with your estate attorney and keep the two consistent.

      What is a heartbeat?

      A small signed message from your pulse key saying you are still here. It is the proof of life. Nothing happens while heartbeats keep arriving.

      Which chains?

      NEAR, Base, Monad and Solana, the last three through NEAR Chain Signatures. The demo uses Base Sepolia, Monad testnet and Solana devnet. Each foreign payout is a fixed amount from the vault’s derived address, which must hold the funds. On testnet those payouts have been signed but none has been relayed yet, because the derived addresses are unfunded.

      Can my heirs see my balance?

      Yes. The heir page lists every vault that names the heir’s account, with the share set aside for them, while you are alive. NEAR is a public ledger, so the plan and balances in the contract are visible to anyone who looks up the vault on-chain.

      What does it cost?

      The hackathon version runs on testnet and is free. Mainnet pricing has not been set; mainnet pricing will be announced with the release.
