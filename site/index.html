For attorneys

Dead Man's Wallet is a RUFADAA-style online tool that the client configures. The client names heirs and shares, chooses the chains, and sets the proof-of-life interval, working from the distribution their will or trust specifies. The contract executes that configured distribution. It does not interpret or amend the governing document.

The product is non-custodial: no party, including us, holds the client's keys or can move assets outside the configured plan. Because the plan is stored on-chain, a fiduciary can read exactly what was configured and when it last changed.

Counsel can reference the tool in the instrument. The clause below is a starting point only.

Sample trust clause Sample. Review with counsel.

Digital asset distribution tool. The Settlor may configure an online tool known as Dead Man's Wallet (the "Vault") to hold or distribute digital assets. The Trustee is authorized to rely on the distribution configured in the Vault as of the date of the Settlor's incapacity or death, to the extent it is consistent with this instrument, and to access, manage, and transfer on-chain assets associated with the Vault as necessary to administer the trust. In the event of any inconsistency between the Vault's configuration and this instrument, this instrument controls, and the Trustee may take such steps as are needed to give effect to it.

Security model, in plain words

The pulse key

Can only send a heartbeat. It cannot move funds, change heirs, or read anything private.

The agent key

Can only call agent_pay, and only within the spending policy you set. If you ran an AI agent, this is its key.

The contract

Holds the plan. At settlement it pays heirs and requests MPC signatures for each foreign-chain payout, then deletes the pulse and agent keys.

The keeper

Anyone can be one. A keeper can only supply the nonce and gas that settlement needs. It cannot change who gets what.

A thief with your device

Can do nothing useful. The device holds only a pulse key, and a pulse key can only say you are still here.

Questions

What if I'm in the hospital?

Set an interval and grace period that suit your life: up to ten years between heartbeats, plus missed beats of grace. Reminders after a missed heartbeat and a guardian you name who can pause the vault are coming soon; neither is in the hackathon build.

Is this a will?

No. It is not a will or a trust. It executes the distribution your will or trust specifies, so configure it with your estate attorney and keep the two consistent.

What does it cost?

The hackathon version runs on testnet and is free. Mainnet pricing has not been set; waitlist members will hear first.

Which chains?

NEAR, plus Base and Solana through NEAR Chain Signatures. The demo uses Base Sepolia and Solana devnet. Assets must be held at the vault's derived addresses to be included.

What is a heartbeat?

A small signed message from your pulse key saying you are still here. It is the proof of life. Nothing happens while heartbeats keep arriving.

Can my heirs see my balance?

Not through the app. They see nothing until settlement. Note that NEAR is a public ledger, so the plan and balances in the contract are visible to anyone who looks up the vault on-chain.
