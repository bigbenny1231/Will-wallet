Your crypto shouldn’t die with you.

While you’re alive, nothing happens. When you’re not, your vault pays the people you chose — on NEAR, Base, Monad and Solana — the way your will or trust says.

The name stays in this browser. Nothing is sent.

Press and hold for 1.5 seconds, or hold Space. Enter starts it at once. It replays vault 4, recorded on testnet; nothing is sent.

On NEAR the contract pays heirs directly, and they sign nothing to receive it. On Base, Monad and Solana, NEAR’s network signs each payout for a keeper to relay.

Now run one yourself.

Three minutes, no wallet. Your browser makes a throwaway account on NEAR testnet and opens a real vault with a 30-second heartbeat.

  1. IAlive. The page beats with a pulse key. Your agent pays an invoice, then obeys a planted message and is refused by the contract.
  2. IISilence. You hold to stop. Two missed beats later, about a minute, anyone may poke the vault.
  3. IIISettlement. Five steps in six transactions: heirs paid, keys deleted, Base and Solana payouts signed by NEAR’s MPC network, the estate DAO step skipped, the Will NFT handed over.

      The security model, in plain words.

      Every key does one thing, and none can change who gets what. The contract account has no full-access key, so nobody, its authors included, can change its code.

      WhoCanCannot
      The pulse key Send a heartbeat. Move funds, change heirs, or read anything private.
      The agent key Call agent_pay within the spending policy you set. If you run an AI agent, this is its key. Pay a receiver outside the allowlist, or exceed the per-payment and daily limits.
      The contract Hold the plan. At settlement, pay heirs, delete the pulse and agent keys, then request an MPC signature for each foreign-chain payout. Move assets outside the configured plan, as deployed.
      A keeper anyone can be one Supply the fees, gas limit and Solana blockhash settlement needs. The contract sets the nonce. Change who gets what.
      A thief with your phone Send heartbeats, which only say you are still here. That can postpone settlement until you remove the key. Move funds or change heirs. The phone holds only a pulse key.
      Us, who built it Nothing the rows above do not allow. The contract account has no full-access key; the lock transaction is on record. Change the code, or move a vault’s funds outside the plan.

The contract executes that configuration. It does not interpret or amend the governing document. Whether it counts as an “online tool” under RUFADAA, or any state’s law, is a question for counsel; we make no such claim.

      Questions

      What if I am alive but offline?

      Choose an interval and grace that suit your life: up to ten years between heartbeats, plus missed beats of grace. A guardian you name can pause the vault once per heartbeat cycle, for up to a length you set. A keeper can post to a Slack or Discord webhook the moment a beat is missed.

      What if I lose my phone, or someone takes it?

      The phone holds a pulse key and nothing else. A thief can only send heartbeats, which postpones settlement until you remove the key from your own account. A lost phone stops sending them, so pair a new one, or send a heartbeat from your wallet, before the grace runs out.

      What if my guardian turns against me, or disappears?

      A guardian can pause once per heartbeat cycle, for at most the length you set. A pause moves no funds, changes no heirs and touches no keys, but while it runs your agent cannot pay. Your next heartbeat ends the pause and allows one more, so a hostile guardian can delay settlement once and can keep switching your agent off until you remove them. A guardian who disappears changes nothing; the vault runs on your heartbeat alone.

      Can it be stopped once it starts?

      Until someone pokes the vault, one heartbeat from you restores it, however late. After the poke it cannot be stopped: the five steps run to the end. By design, the grace period is the cancel window, so choose a generous one.

      Is this a will?

      No. It is not a will or a trust. It executes the distribution your will or trust specifies, so configure it with your estate attorney and keep the two consistent.

      What is a heartbeat?

      A small signed message from your pulse key saying you are still here. Nothing happens while heartbeats keep arriving.

      Which chains?

      NEAR, Base, Monad and Solana, the last three through NEAR Chain Signatures; the demo uses Base Sepolia, Monad testnet and Solana devnet. Each foreign payout is a fixed amount from the vault’s derived address, which must hold the funds: NEAR’s MPC network signs it at settlement, and a keeper relays it and records its hash on the vault.

      Can my heirs see my balance?

      Yes. The heir page lists every vault that names the heir’s account, with the share set aside for them, while you are alive. NEAR is a public ledger, so the plan and balances in the contract are visible to anyone who looks.
